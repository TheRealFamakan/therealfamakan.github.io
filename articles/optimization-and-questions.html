Why optimization is more than finding the minimum

Published August 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Optimization is often introduced as the search for the smallest or largest value. In practice, it is a language for making choices when resources are limited and objectives compete.

Constraints tell the real story

A solution is meaningful only inside its constraints. A route that is mathematically short may be impossible because of capacity, timing, or uncertainty. Constraints are not an inconvenience added after the model; they describe the world the model must respect.

Trade-offs are part of the answer

Many problems have no perfect solution. Improving one objective can make another worse. A good model exposes this trade-off so that a human decision-maker can choose deliberately.

The question comes first

Before selecting an algorithm, we need to ask what success means. The most sophisticated solver cannot repair an objective that does not represent the decision we actually care about.
